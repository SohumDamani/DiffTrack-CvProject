TL;DR: Generated videos by video diffusion transformer (DiT) with emergent temporal correspondence

Overview of DiffTrack: DiffTrack reveals how Video Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) establish temporal correspondences during video generation. Given a prompt and starting points in the first frame, DiffTrack tracks how individual points align across subsequent frames in video DiTs, enabling the extraction of coherent motion trajectories from both generated and real-world videos in a zero-shot manner. As practical applications, DiffTrack also enables 1) zero-shot point tracking and 2) motion-enhanced video generation with a novel guidance method.

Analysis of Temporal Matching in CogVideoX-5B

  • Representation selection: Query-key matching achieves higher accuracy than intermediate feature matching, providing better representations for temporal matching.
  • Layer-wise analysis: The harmonic mean of query-key matching across layers and timesteps reveals that temporal correspondence is primarily governed by a limited set of layers.
  • Noise-level analysis: Temporal matching improves as noise decreases, with early timesteps relying more on text embeddings and self-frame attention, while later timesteps shift towards cross-frame attention for enhanced coherence.

DiffTrack for Zero-Shot Point Tracking

Quantitative comparison on the TAP-Vid datasets: Video DiTs combined with DiffTrack outperform all vision foundation models trained on single images and self-supervised models trained on two-view images or videos for zero-shot tracking.

Analysis Framework: DiffTrack

Evaluation Dataset Curation

We collect two distinct datasets: (a) an object dataset for dynamic object-centric videos, and (b) a scene dataset for static scenes with camera motion. Each dataset includes 50 text prompts, with 50 videos generated per prompt using CogVideoX-2B. To assess temporal matching, we predefine starting points in the first frame and obtain pseudo ground-truth trajectories using an off-the-shelf tracking method, CoTracker.

Temporal Correspondence Estimation

We evaluate temporal matching by extracting pointwise correspondences across video frames using latent feature descriptors from a video diffusion transformer (DiT).


Matching Cost Computation:
At each timestep \( t \) and layer \( l \), we compute the matching cost between the descriptors of the first frame and the \( j \)-th frame (\( j \in \{2, \dots, 1+f\} \)):

\[ \mathbf{C}^{1,j}_{t,l} = \mathtt{Softmax} \left( \frac{\mathbf{D}_{t,l}^1 (\mathbf{D}_{t,l}^j)^T}{\sqrt{d}} \right) \]

where \( \mathbf{D}_{t,l}^1 \) and \( \mathbf{D}_{t,l}^j \) are latent descriptors, and \( d \) is the channel dimension.


Correspondence Estimation:
Matched points are obtained by selecting the highest scoring location for each query:

\[ \mathbf{p}^j_{t,l} = \underset{\mathbf{x} \in \Omega}{\mathtt{Argmax}} \ \mathbf{C}^{1,j}_{t,l}(\mathbf{p}^1, \mathbf{x}) \]

where \( \mathbf{p}^1 \) is the point in the first frame and \( \Omega \) is the spatial domain.


Trajectory Reconstruction:
The temporal motion track is formed by concatenating matched points and interpolating back to RGB space:

\[ \hat{\mathbf{T}}_{t,l} = \mathtt{Interp}\left(\mathtt{Concat}(\mathbf{p}^1, \mathbf{p}^2_{t,l}, \dots, \mathbf{p}^{1+f}_{t,l})\right) \]

Evaluation Metrics

To evaluate temporal consistency in video generation, we propose three complementary metrics:

  • Matching Accuracy: Measures how precisely points are aligned across frames.
  • Confidence Score: Measures how strongly each point attends to its match in the attention map.
  • Attention Score: Measures how strongly cross-frame interactions influence during video generation

These metrics capture different aspects of matching. High accuracy does not guarantee influence on generation if attention score is low, and high confidence does not ensure correct matching. To jointly assess three things, we compute the harmonic mean of the normalized scores, highlighting regions where all accuracy, confidence and attention are high.

🎓 CV Final Project — Reproduction & Ablation Study

Sohum Damani  ·  TAP-Vid DAVIS  ·  CogVideoX-2B  ·  Zero-Shot Point Tracking

We reproduced the paper's baseline, ran 13 ablation experiments, and confirmed 2 strengths and 3 paper-grounded limitations. All experiment logs are committed to the GitHub repo. Tracking videos are hosted on Google Drive (~111 MP4s).

✅ Strength 1 — Baseline Faithfully Reproduced

We ran the paper's recommended configuration (layer=17, ts=49 = paper's t=1) on 2 TAP-Vid DAVIS videos and obtained delta_avg = 47.0, compared to the paper's reported 46.3 on the full 30-video set — within 1.5%. The method tracks real physical points zero-shot, with no training.

✅ Strength 2 — Timestep Sensitivity Independently Verified

We ran 6 timestep values (ts=1, 5, 10, 20, 30, 49) and traced the full sensitivity curve. This independently replicates paper Figure 4(c): performance rises from zero at high noise, peaks slightly before the final denoising step (ts=30 → 47.8 > ts=49 → 47.0), and collapses completely at ts=1 (0.0 on all 4 videos).

Key insight: The "ts=30 > ts=49" ordering independently confirms the paper's claim of "slight degradation toward the final denoising steps" — something we verified in our own data, not just taken from the paper.

⚠️ Limitation 1 — Complete Failure at Wrong Timestep

At ts=1 (scheduler noise level t=980 out of 1000), every single metric is exactly 0.0 across all 4 test videos. The transformer receives clean video latents but is conditioned on the near-maximum-noise timestep — a distribution mismatch that destroys all correspondence features. This is confirmed by paper Figure 4(c) and Section 4.

✅ ts=49 — Tracking Works

delta_avg = 47.0

❌ ts=1 — Complete Failure

delta_avg = 0.00 (all 4 videos, all metrics)

Mechanism (code-verified): With no DDIM inversion (our experiments don't use --do_inversion), the pipeline encodes the real video to clean VAE latents, then runs the transformer conditioned at timestep t=980. This is inconsistent with training (model expects heavily noisy latents at t=980). Features collapse entirely. The effective operating range is ts=30–49 only.

⚠️ Limitation 2 — Positional Bias in Layer 8 (Paper Figure 6)

Layer 8 achieves delta_avg = 41.9 vs the optimal layer 17's ~46.4 — a 10% deficit. Paper Figure 6 explicitly identifies this layer as having high tracking confidence but low accuracy, because RoPE positional embeddings cause the model to match the same spatial location rather than the actual object.

✅ Layer 17 (optimal) — delta_avg = 47.0

⚠️ Layer 8 (positional bias) — delta_avg = 41.9

Paper grounding: Figure 6 in the paper explicitly identifies this effect — PCA of queries/keys in layer 8 reveals dominance of positional cues, and tracked points cluster at original spatial positions rather than true correspondences. Our quantitative result (41.9 vs 46.4) confirms this.

⚠️ Limitation 3 — Diffuse Attention in Final Layers (Paper Figure A.18)

Layer 29 achieves delta_avg = 38.0 vs layer 17's ~46.4 — an 18% deficit. Paper Figure A.18 explicitly names layers 8, 24, and 29 as the bottom-3 performers, showing "diffuse and scattered attention" that cannot localize corresponding points. We tested both layers 8 and 29 from this list and confirm the degradation.

✅ Layer 17 (optimal) — delta_avg = 47.0

⚠️ Layer 29 (bottom-3, diffuse) — delta_avg = 38.0

Paper grounding: Figure A.18 caption: "Bottom-3 layers (l=8, 24, 29) display diffuse and scattered attention." Layer 17 is in the top-3 (l=13, 17, 21) with "sharp and precise localization." The 18% gap between layer 29 and layer 17 is consistent with the paper's visualization.

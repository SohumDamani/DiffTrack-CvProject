01 — Overview

Zero-Shot Point Tracking from
Frozen Video DiTs

DiffTrack reveals how Video Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) establish temporal correspondences during video generation. Given starting points in the first frame, DiffTrack tracks how individual points move across subsequent frames — entirely zero-shot, no fine-tuning required.

TL;DR: Generated videos by video diffusion transformer (DiT) with emergent temporal correspondence. DiffTrack enables both zero-shot point tracking and motion-enhanced video generation via a novel CAG guidance method.

  • Zero-shot point tracking — no training on video data. State-of-the-art on TAP-Vid.
  • Motion-enhanced generation — CAG leverages temporal attention to increase motion.
  • Beats DINOv2 & VFS — outperforms vision foundation models on DAVIS and Kinetics.
  • Representation selection: Query-key matching achieves higher accuracy than intermediate feature matching.
  • Layer-wise analysis: Temporal correspondence is governed by a limited set of mid-network layers.
  • Noise-level analysis: Matching improves as noise decreases; later timesteps shift toward cross-frame attention.

Video DiTs combined with DiffTrack outperform all vision foundation models trained on single images and self-supervised models trained on two-view images or videos for zero-shot point tracking on TAP-Vid DAVIS & Kinetics.

03 — Method

Analysis Framework

Evaluation Dataset Curation

Two distinct datasets: (a) object dataset for dynamic object-centric videos, (b) scene dataset for static scenes with camera motion. 50 prompts each, 50 videos per prompt (CogVideoX-2B). Pseudo ground-truth via CoTracker.

Evaluation Metrics

  • Matching Accuracy — how precisely points are aligned across frames
  • Confidence Score — how strongly each point attends to its match
  • Attention Score — how strongly cross-frame interactions influence generation

Combined via harmonic mean — highlighting regions where all three metrics are simultaneously high.

Temporal Correspondence Estimation

Matching Cost at timestep \(t\), layer \(l\):

\[\mathbf{C}^{1,j}_{t,l} = \mathtt{Softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{D}_{t,l}^1(\mathbf{D}_{t,l}^j)^T}{\sqrt{d}}\right)\]

Correspondence Estimation:

\[\mathbf{p}^j_{t,l} = \underset{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega}{\mathtt{Argmax}}\ \mathbf{C}^{1,j}_{t,l}(\mathbf{p}^1,\mathbf{x})\]

Trajectory Reconstruction:

\[\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{t,l} = \mathtt{Interp}\!\left(\mathtt{Concat}(\mathbf{p}^1, \mathbf{p}^2_{t,l},\ldots,\mathbf{p}^{1+f}_{t,l})\right)\]

Paper-Grounded Limitations Confirmed
⚠️ Limitation 1 — Complete Failure at Wrong Timestep (ts=1) delta_avg = 0.0

ts=49 (paper t=1) · delta_avg = 47.0 ✅

ts=1 (scheduler t=980) · delta_avg = 0.00 ❌

Mechanism (code-verified)

Our experiments omit --do_inversion. The pipeline encodes video to clean VAE latents, then runs the transformer conditioned at t=980 (scheduler noise scale — near-maximum noise). The model was trained to process heavily-noisy latents at t=980; receiving clean latents creates a distribution mismatch that destroys all correspondence features. Confirmed by paper Figure 4(c) and Section 4. Effective operating range: ts=30–49 only.
